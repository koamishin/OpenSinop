OpenSinop brings receipts, budgets, bills, and review decisions into one place.
The useful question is not just “How much is in the wallet?” It is **“Which approved
bills can this department cover without using money set aside for something else?”**

## Start with a familiar example

Suppose a department has collected fees and needs to pay for an internet renewal and
laboratory supplies. Some of the receipts are restricted to a scholarship fund.

The finance officer records the received fees. Another staff member checks the report
and its restrictions. Staff then select the two approved bills and capture the current
budget and cash position.

OpenSinop checks each selected bill in due date order. A bill can be proposed for review
only when its evidence is approved and both remaining budget and usable cash cover it.
If not, the plan shows a hold and the failed checks.

**The planner does not choose new bills or move money.** Staff choose the bill list;
the application checks that list. Optional AI advice is a separate explanation or
classification, not permission to spend.

## Four decisions that must not be confused

### 1. Is this a valid collection record?

The reviewer compares the receipt or report with the institution's records and checks
restrictions. Approval means the recorded evidence was reviewed. It is not independent
bank verification and does not transfer funds.

### 2. Does this spending plan make sense?

The supervisor checks the chosen bills, available allocation, and usable cash. Accepting
the plan records agreement with that exact proposal. It does not approve a payment.

### 3. May this exact payment be submitted?

In the reviewed vendor path, an enrolled payment reviewer checks the recipient, amount,
network, and reserved capacity. The reviewer supplies a fresh password and authenticator
code. A valid network approval creates durable submission work; an enabled worker may
process it afterward. The review screen itself does not call the payment provider.

### 4. Did the expected transfer happen?

The payment provider may accept a request before an onchain transaction exists.
OpenSinop must check successful movement of the expected amount between the expected
addresses. A provider ID or transaction hash alone is not settlement evidence.

## Find your workspace

Use your institution's application address, not this documentation website. What you
can see depends on your role and permissions.

| Workspace | What you do there |
| --- | --- |
| **Finance dashboard** (`/finance`) | See collection reviews, planning runs, payment decisions, and operating health. |
| **Collections** | Record receipts and reports; review them independently; reconcile eligible student pilot receipts. |
| **Finance Supervisor** | Capture a planning snapshot, inspect results, review a proposal, and prepare an eligible bill for payment review. |
| **Payment Approvals** | Inspect reserved payments, record a decision, and check reviewer readiness. |
| **Treasury Wallet** | Inspect the configured wallet and bill blockers. Balance refresh is not payment approval. |
| **Institution policies / Policy evaluations** | Define policy content and request advice against recorded sources. |
| **Admin** (`/admin`) | Manage installation settings, accounts, and records. Access is separate from finance duties. |
| **Student screens** | View the student's own accounts, wallet, payments, and assistance features. |

## What changes, and what stays outside

- **Recording a manual collection** creates evidence. It does not receive new money or change a bank balance.
- **Planning** calculates remaining budget and cash from supplied evidence. It reserves nothing.
- **Reserving capacity** records an application hold. It does not lock the Circle wallet against outside use.
- **Verified testnet movement** proves movement on the test network. It does not settle a local bank payable.

The ordinary reviewed path keeps these steps separate. The legacy bridge is now
read-only: it reports missing review/authorization and never submits funds. Historical
bridge reviewer records still require audit; they do not prove independent consent.
See [Current limits](../../institution/safety-readiness/#read-only-bridge-and-assistance-boundaries).

## Background runtime

Saving evidence can create a background job. A **worker** processes jobs; a **scheduler**
periodically finds work needing dispatch or recovery. An open browser is not required.

Planning is enabled in application configuration. The usual submission and student
payment switches default off, but the experimental bridge defaults on. The stock Docker
Compose web service starts neither a general queue worker nor the scheduler.

Operators: use [Run background services](../operations/) before starting either process.
